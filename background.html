About Our Local Artists

Peter Mpofu

I was born and raised here in Gaborone, and the city has always been a huge part of who I am. Growing up, I was fascinated by the colors, textures, and energy of everyday life the markets, the streets, the mix of old and new. I remember spending hours drawing little sketches of the things I saw around me, from street vendors to the architecture of old buildings. It was my escape and my way of understanding the world. I officially got into arts and crafts when I joined a local youth center in my teens, where I learned painting, beadwork, and some basic sculpture. From there, I started experimenting on my own, blending traditional Botswanan motifs with modern ideas. For me, art is more than just a craft; it’s a conversation with the world. I draw inspiration from the textures of everyday life, nature, music, and human emotions. What keeps me going is the belief that every stroke and every sketch can evoke feeling, spark thought, or connect someone to an idea they didn’t know existed. Today, I focus on creating pieces that capture Gaborone’s evolving cultural scene while telling stories about identity, community, and the beauty of the ordinary. My vision is to continue exploring and pushing boundaries, while inspiring others to see the extraordinary in everyday life.

John Sibanda

I come from Jackalas 1, a small village where life moves at its own rhythm. My childhood was shaped by nature, by stories told around fires, and by the close connections we have in the community. I was always curious about colors and shapes, often making little crafts from clay, sticks, and anything I could find. My love for arts and crafts really took off when I joined a community program that taught traditional weaving, pottery, and basketry. Seeing how ordinary materials could be transformed into something beautiful inspired me to experiment more. Over the years, I began blending these traditional techniques with my own style, capturing everyday moments, village life, and our heritage through my work. I am inspired by contrasts light and shadow, chaos and calm, tradition and modernity. People, their stories, and the world around me constantly push me to create. What fuels me is the endless possibility of expression, the chance to turn emotion into color, movement, and form. Now, I focus on preserving our culture while innovating within traditional crafts. My vision is to showcase Jackalas 1’s heritage to a wider audience and inspire younger generations to explore arts and crafts while staying connected to their roots

Xolile artworks

I grew up in Gabane, surrounded by both the community and the natural landscapes of the Kweneng District. From a young age, I loved observing people their movements, expressions, and small gestures and translating those into sketches. I also spent time with local craftspeople, learning about beadwork, pottery, and traditional decorations. That’s when I realized I could turn observation into creation. My formal entry into arts and crafts came through school workshops and community projects, where I learned to mix techniques, experiment with colors, and develop my own style. Art became my way of capturing small but meaningful moments, and turning them into stories that others could see and feel. I’m inspired by the quiet, unnoticed moments in life the play of light, the rhythm of nature, and the subtle emotions of people. What keeps me going is the endless curiosity to see the world differently and the hope that my art can make someone pause, feel, or remember something they had forgotten. Today, I focus on creating works that reflect both the people and landscapes of Gabane, capturing emotion and narrative in every piece. My goal is to continue exploring human expression and nature through art, while connecting communities and inspiring reflection through my work
